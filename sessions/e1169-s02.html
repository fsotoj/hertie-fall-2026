The four concepts, and what separates them

Written from the literature rather than from the deck, which is not published yet. Orientation to be corrected against what is actually said in class.

Becker's model. Offending is a portfolio choice under risk: commit if the expected gain beats the expected cost, and the expected cost is the probability of apprehension p times the severity of the sanction f. Two things fall out of that product. Deterrence works on p × f, so the same deterrent can be bought with certainty or with severity. And under risk aversion the response to p is larger than the response to f, which is the standard argument for certain-but-moderate punishment over rare-but-harsh punishment. Since enforcement costs money and long sentences are comparatively cheap to legislate, the policy question is which margin buys more deterrence per euro.

General against specific deterrence. General deterrence changes the behaviour of people who were never punished; specific deterrence changes the behaviour of the person who was. They can point in opposite directions, because punishment also depreciates outside options and builds criminal networks. Specific deterrence coming out negative while general deterrence is positive is a coherent result, not a contradiction.

Incapacitation. A prisoner commits no street crime while inside. This is not deterrence: it requires no behavioural response at all. Both mechanisms cut crime when sentences lengthen, which is exactly why separating them is hard, and why the empirical papers lean on designs where only one of the two channels can move.

Displacement. Crime prevented at the treated place, time or target may reappear elsewhere. Where it does, the local effect a clean design estimates is larger than the effect on total crime. This is the standard reason a well-identified evaluation can still be the wrong input to a policy decision, and it is a fair question to have ready for every paper presented from session 3 on.

Two method flags worth carrying in

  • Draca, Machin and Witt is the cleanest design of the group. The deployment surge after the July 2005 attacks moved officers across London boroughs for six weeks for reasons unrelated to local crime trends, which makes it a difference-in-differences with a genuine shock.
  • Levitt (1997) is the famous one and also the cautionary one. McCrary's 2002 comment in the AER found a weighting error that removed the result, and Levitt replied using a different instrument. Worth knowing on its own terms: "the instrument is fine, the code was not" is a failure mode that no identification argument protects against.

What you should be able to do afterwards

  • Write down the Becker condition and say why p and f are not interchangeable in practice
  • Give a case where specific and general deterrence pull in opposite directions
  • Name a design that identifies incapacitation without deterrence contaminating it
  • Say what displacement does to the external validity of a well-identified local estimate
