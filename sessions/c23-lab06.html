What lab 5 covered, and what it left

Lab 5 was the first half of lecture 5 plus integration: the mean and variance of the Uniform and the Exponential by integration, a density of the form cx² on [0, 3], Monte Carlo integration, and the Normal by standardising, with memorylessness and Riemann sums as optional extras. It left out joint densities, covariance and correlation, and all of lecture 6. Unlike lab 4, it says nothing about what comes next.

A reasonable guess, from how labs 3 to 5 were built (simulate, then compare with the exact answer): the law of large numbers as a running average; the central limit theorem as a histogram of sample means from a skewed distribution; the likelihood evaluated on a grid and laid over a Beta density; maximum likelihood found numerically and compared with the closed forms. Possibly covariance from simulated data, which lecture 5 left untouched. That is an inference.

Three traps worth knowing before you start. NumPy's exponential sampler takes the scale 1/λ and its Normal sampler the standard deviation. np.var divides by n, which is the Normal maximum likelihood estimate, while np.cov divides by n − 1. And optimisers minimise, so hand them the negative log-likelihood.

Checks worth doing by hand first

  • The mean of 36 Exponential(1) draws: the CLT puts P(mean > 1.2) at 0.115; the exact value is 0.118.
  • The black box, 14 wins in 41: the likelihood peaks at 14/41 ≈ 0.341, the mode of Beta(15, 28), not its mean 15/43.
  • Saves of 2 in 4, 6 in 10 and 1 in 2: the estimate is 9/16 = 0.5625, not 0.533, the average of the rates.
  • Data 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9: mean 5, maximum likelihood variance 4, sample variance 4.57.

What you should be able to do afterwards

  • Show the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem by simulation
  • Evaluate a likelihood on a grid and find its peak
  • Find a maximum likelihood estimate numerically and check it against the formula
