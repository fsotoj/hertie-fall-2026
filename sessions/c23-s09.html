How it connects to lecture 8

Lecture 8 ended with the SVD as an X-ray of the data: small singular values mean redundancy, so the data fit in fewer dimensions. Session 9 turns that into a method. Maximise the variance of a projection subject to a unit-length direction, form the Lagrangian, set its gradient to zero, and the answer is an eigenvector equation. So principal component loadings are the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, and for centred data they are the right singular vectors from lecture 8.

Be able to explain afterwards

  • What a gradient is, and the rules ∇(bᵀx) = b and ∇(xᵀAx) = 2Ax for symmetric A, by analogy with one variable.
  • Why, at a constrained optimum, the objective's gradient is parallel to the constraint's, and what the multiplier λ measures.
  • PCA in one sentence; loadings, scores, proportion of variance explained, the scree plot.
  • Why to centre, when to scale, and why signs of loadings can flip.

Self-checks: the minimum of x + y on the unit circle is −√2 at x = y = −1/√2. For covariance [[2, 1], [1, 2]] the first component is (1, 1)/√2 and explains 75% of the variance.
