What lab 4 says comes next

"From here on, calculus is a tool rather than a topic. Everything we do with continuous random variables in the next lab rests on it."

Lab 4's solutions add that for a continuous variable the CDF climbs smoothly instead of in jumps, and that its derivative, which plays the role of the PMF, "is exactly where the next section, and the next lab, are heading". So the topic is stated. The contents are not.

A reasonable guess from how labs 3 and 4 were built (simulate, then compare with the exact answer): densities as derivatives of CDFs and probabilities as areas; sampling the Uniform, Normal and Exponential and laying the density over the histogram; a joint density taken apart into marginals; the law of large numbers as a running average and the central limit theorem as a histogram of sample means. That is an inference. The environment has not changed: NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib and SciPy.

One trap worth knowing before you start. NumPy's exponential sampler takes the scale 1/λ, not the rate λ, and its Normal sampler takes the standard deviation, not the variance.

Catch-up: lab 4, exercise 4

The TA said on Moodle that the Thursday 12:00 group skipped it by mistake, and pointed to the released solutions. It is lecture 4 material, so it is also quiz 2 material.

The worked function. f(x) = x³ − 3x² + 1 has f′ = 3x(x − 2) and f″ = 6(x − 1). The sign of f′ goes +, −, + around 0 and 2, so there is a local maximum at 0 (value 1) and a local minimum at 2 (value −3); the second derivative agrees (−6 and 6). Concavity flips at x = 1, the inflection point. Neither extremum is global.

Doing it numerically. Apply np.gradient once for f′ and again for f″, find where f′ changes sign on the grid, and read the sign of f″ there.

Why f″ = 0 says nothing. x⁴, −x⁴ and x³ all have f′(0) = f″(0) = 0, and they are a minimum, a maximum and neither. Only the first derivative test, which reads the sign of f′ on both sides, tells them apart.

The optional preview. Maximising the likelihood p³(1 − p)⁷ through its logarithm gives p = 3/10, the observed share: the first maximum likelihood estimate, and the topic of session 6.

What you should be able to do afterwards

  • Turn a CDF into a density and a density into probabilities, by hand and numerically
  • Simulate the Uniform, Normal and Exponential with the right parameters
  • Check a simulated histogram against the exact density
  • Show the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem by simulation
