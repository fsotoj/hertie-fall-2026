What the reading does

Lecture 4 ended by saying why it existed: a density is the derivative of a CDF, and a probability is an area or a volume. This lecture is where that gets used.

Densities. A continuous variable puts probability zero on every single value, so the PMF is useless and the CDF takes over. Its derivative, the density f, plays the PMF's role: probabilities are integrals of f, expectation and LOTUS swap the sum for an integral, and linearity is unchanged. A density can exceed 1; it is probability per unit length.

Three named distributions. The Uniform, where probability is proportional to length, with variance (b − a)²/12. The Normal: standardise with Z = (X − μ)/σ, then use Φ, which has no formula because e−x²/2 has no elementary antiderivative; 68, 95 and 99.7% within one, two and three standard deviations, and 1.96 for a 95% interval. The Exponential, the waiting time with mean 1/λ, whose defining property is memorylessness: having waited tells you nothing about the remaining wait.

Joint densities. Lecture 3's joint table with integrals: marginals by integrating the other variable out, conditionals by rescaling a slice, independence when the density factors. Then covariance, which supplies the missing term in Var(X + Y) = Var X + Var Y + 2 Cov(X, Y), and correlation, its unit-free version. Independent variables are uncorrelated, but not the reverse, except inside a multivariate Normal.

The two limit theorems. The law of large numbers says a sample mean settles on the true mean, provided the mean exists (the St. Petersburg game in lab 4 is the case where it does not). The central limit theorem says how it fluctuates on the way: approximately Normal with standard deviation σ/√n, whatever the shape of the underlying variable. It is why a Binomial looks Normal, and it is the basis of every standard error later in the course.

What you should be able to do afterwards

  • Explain why a continuous variable has probability zero at every point
  • Check a density, and get probabilities, the mean and the variance from it
  • Use the Uniform, Normal and Exponential, and say which story each fits
  • Standardise a Normal and read Φ, with the 68–95–99.7 rule and 1.96
  • Get marginals and conditionals from a joint density, and test independence
  • Compute a covariance and a correlation, and expand the variance of a sum
  • Give dependent variables with zero correlation, and name where that cannot happen
  • State the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem, with their conditions
