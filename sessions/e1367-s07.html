Who she is

Postdoctoral researcher at the University of Zurich (Department of Business Administration, Management and Economics of Digital Transformation) since 2022, and a visiting researcher at the WZB in Berlin since February 2026. PhD in Business Economics from KU Leuven (2025); before the PhD she worked at McKinsey and at Copenhagen Economics. She describes her research in two lines: online news media (how aggregators, competition, regulation and financing shape news) and online toxicity (its causes and its consequences for content creation). Source: her website and CV, read 11 October.

Her research, and the likely topics

Ranked by how likely each seems to be the talk. A guess.

1. "Gender Bias, Feedback, and Productivity" (job market paper, version of 17 November 2025). In November 2021 YouTube stopped showing public dislike counts. Before the change, videos by women received 43% more dislikes than videos by men; afterwards women's dislikes fell 21% more than men's, and the gender gap closed. Women then produced 8.4% more videos and their viewership rose 15.5% more than men's. The fall in dislikes is concentrated at the top of the distribution, which fits YouTube's stated aim of curbing "dislike attacks". A placebo in Korea and Japan, where there was no gender gap before, shows no effect. Design: difference-in-differences comparing women (more treated) with men (less treated), on 1,808 individual-run channels whose creator gender comes from a face classifier. Source: the paper's abstract and introduction.

2. "Substituting Away? The Effect of Platform Bargaining Regulation on Content Display" (working paper). Australia's News Media Bargaining Code forces platforms to negotiate payment with domestic publishers. On Google News data, the Code raised the share of large foreign publishers and lowered that of major domestic ones. Source: abstract only.

Work in progress (titles only): the effect of Facebook's 2021 news ban in Australia on news consumption and voting, and media competition and news quality under bargaining regulation, both with Mattia Nardotto and co-authors.

How it connects to the course

  • Session 9, hate speech: the dislikes paper and the session 9 debate paper both treat online toxicity as something a platform's design can turn up or down, and both measure it with machine-learning classifiers. Her paper thanks the first author of the session 9 debate paper.
  • Sessions 5 and 6, financing and ownership: the bargaining-code paper is about who pays for news and whose news platforms choose to show.

Questions worth having ready

  • On the dislikes paper: demand for women's videos rose about twice as much as their supply (15.5% against 8.4%). Can the paper separate a creator channel (women post more because they face less harassment) from a viewer channel (audiences are no longer put off by a visible pile-on)?
  • On the bargaining code: did the shift toward foreign publishers change what kind of news readers saw, for example local against international or political coverage?
  • For any talk on toxicity: how sensitive are the results to the classifier's threshold, and do profanity and identity-based hate behave differently?
